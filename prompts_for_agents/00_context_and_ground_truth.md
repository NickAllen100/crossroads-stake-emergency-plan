## 2. Top Hazard Profile (Saratoga Springs / 1-Mile Radius)

Based on the **Mountainland Association of Governments (MAG) Pre-Disaster Mitigation Plan** and Saratoga Springs Fire & Rescue assessments:

1. **Earthquake (Wasatch Fault Zone - Utah Valley Segment / Utah Lake Faults):**
   * High ground-shaking probability.
   * Liquefaction hazard along low-lying areas near Utah Lake / Jordan River outfall.
   * Severe risk of ruptured natural gas mains, severed water supply mains, and bridge disruption across the Jordan River (disconnecting Saratoga Springs from Lehi/I-15).
2. **Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI) Wildfire (Lake Mountain Slope):**
   * Saratoga Springs ranks in the top 5% nationally for wildfire risk (e.g., 2020 Knolls Fire forced 13,000 evacuations; 2012 Dump Fire).
   * Steep dry cheatgrass and sagebrush on Lake Mountain slopes directly west/southwest of the neighborhood.
   * High winds blowing downslope can push fire into Aspen Hills/Harvest Hills subdivisions in under 45 minutes.
3. **Severe Winter Blizzard & Power Grid Freeze:**
   * Temperatures dropping below 0°F with sustained sub-freezing winds.
   * Overloaded electric substations causing multi-day blackouts.
   * High risk of burst culinary water pipes, hypothermia for vulnerable residents, and impassable unplowed roads.
4. **Severe High Windstorms (Microbursts & Gap Winds):**
   * Frequent 60–90+ mph wind gusts funneling between the Traverse Mountains and Lake Mountain.
   * Downed trees, utility poles, flying debris, damaged roofs, flipped trampolines/sheds, and blinding dust storms on Redwood Road.
5. **Hazardous Materials (Hazmat) Spill on Redwood Road (SR-68):**
   * SR-68 is a primary tanker freight corridor for hazardous industrial chemicals, liquefied gases, and petroleum between Salt Lake and Utah counties.
   * A rollover or chemical cloud release within 0.5 miles of Aspen Hills Blvd requires immediate shelter-in-place or reverse-evacuation.
6. **Culinary Water System Main Failure / Contamination:**
   * Dual water system: Saratoga Springs operates both a culinary (potable) system and a separate pressurized irrigation (secondary) system pumped from Utah Lake.
   * In a major pipe failure or cross-contamination incident, culinary drinking water becomes non-potable; high-elevation pressure zones lose pressure immediately.
